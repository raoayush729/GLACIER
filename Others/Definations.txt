==========================================================REGEX String====================================================================
(r"(\d+(?:\.\d+)?)")[0]

Meaning:

( --- opening of a group
) --- closing of a group
r --- raw string(\n)
\ --- It introduces a special regex instruction. (\d,\w etc)
\d --- one digit(1,2,3,4,5,6,7,8,9)
\d+ --- one or more digit (1,12,234,543,6546456)
( --- This starts another group.
) --- closes the inner group
?: --- (?: ... ) = group something together, but don't create a separate extracted result for that group.
\. --- this means an actual decimal point
\d+ --- again one or more digit
? --- at the end ? is  optional — this part may or may not exist.



So:
\d+(?:\.\d+)?
can match:
4
4.6
25
25.75 


[0] --- df["Rating"].str.extract(...)

Pandas extracts the matching part.

So the result is not just one value.

It is a Pandas DataFrame containing the extracted results.

Think of it like:

        0
0      4.6
1        4
2        5
3      3.5

◆ df.itertuples()

for row in df.itertuples(index=False, name=None):
    rows = tuple(row)

This is a Pandas DataFrame method.

Its job is:

Go through the DataFrame one row at a time and give you each row.

Suppose your DataFrame is:

Title          Rating       Price
-----------------------------------
Product A       4.5         999
Product B       4.2         799
Product C       4.8        1299

Then:

df.itertuples(index=False, name=None)

will give us the rows one by one.

Conceptually:

row 1 → Product A, 4.5, 999
row 2 → Product B, 4.2, 799
row 3 → Product C, 4.8, 1299


Why name=None?

itertuples() can return named tuples.

For example, without name=None, Pandas can conceptually give you something like:

Pandas(Title="Product A", Rating=4.5, Price=999)

We don't need that.

We simply want:

("Product A", 4.5, 999)

So:

name=None

means:

Give me ordinary tuples rather than named tuples.
